01About

What the work is for

Most of my work sits at the seam between a treatment working and a person being okay. Checkpoint inhibitors have changed what survival looks like in melanoma, and they have left a second problem behind, made of cardiac and neurologic toxicity, fear of recurrence, and a quality-of-life burden that trial endpoints were never designed to capture. Measuring that second problem is what I spend most of my time on.

The method usually takes the same shape. I start from an under-structured record, whether patient narratives, national surveillance data, an expression matrix, or hours of surgical video, and ask whether it holds a signal that survives contact with a new population. Often the honest answer is that it does not, and reporting that is part of the work rather than a failure of it.

    Toward Human-Centered Survivorship Intelligence: A PNI-Grounded Data-Driven Framework for Melanoma

    Bhadra S, Pichappan S, Vatsa A

    IEEE International Conference on Intelligent Systems and Interdisciplinary Applications (ISAIA 2026) · Montclair, New Jersey

    Proposes a way to predict which melanoma survivors will carry lasting burden, grounded in psychoneuroimmunology rather than treatment history, which the evidence shows is a poor guide to who struggles afterward. Five data domains (immune and endocrine biomarkers, neurocognitive testing, validated psychological instruments, socioeconomic indicators, and wearable physiology) map onto a specified causal DAG, gathered through a dual-pathway design that tests whether biomarkers add anything beyond patient-reported and wearable data. Clustering, gradient boosting with interpretability, and structural equation modeling combine into a proposed Melanoma Survivorship Risk Score, which needs multi-site validation before it means anything clinically.

    What it took to learn Three things
    • Psychoneuroimmunology, far enough to map five data domains onto a single causal structure.
    • Directed acyclic graphs, and what it means to commit to a causal structure before fitting anything.
    • Structural equation modeling, and how to check for construct overlap between validated psychological instruments.
    Method notes Two
    1. A causal DAG, not a feature dumpEach data domain is mapped onto a directed acyclic graph whose pathways and interactions are written down in advance, so the framework has a stated causal structure that can be shown wrong rather than an unordered pile of features.
    2. Dual-pathway collectionThe design tests whether immune and endocrine biomarkers add predictive value beyond patient-reported outcomes and wearable data instead of assuming they do. Clustering, gradient boosting with interpretability, and structural equation modeling each carry defined outcome thresholds and a safeguard against construct overlap between instruments.

    A Comprehensive Review on Melanoma Consumers: Neuroscience, Survivorship, and the Burden of Engagement

    Bhadra S, Pichappan S, Vatsa A

    16th IEEE Integrated STEM Education Conference (ISEC ’26) · Princeton University

    Immune checkpoint inhibitors turned advanced melanoma from a fast-fatal diagnosis into one with durable remission, and in doing so created a population of long-term survivors whose needs run well past tumor surveillance. The review draws clinical, quality-of-life, and patient-engagement studies together into four connected burdens: physical and neurocognitive, with cognitive deficits in 41% of survivors and neurologic immune-related adverse events in 1.5 to 7.2% of treated patients; psychological, with 35% meeting criteria for cancer-related PTSD and fear of recurrence structured around scan dates; socioeconomic, through financial toxicity that falls hardest on younger survivors; and systemic, where 36% need mental health support and 7% receive it. It also names the paradox of the melanoma consumer, the survivor who joins research and advocacy and gains agency while taking on a new cognitive load, and argues that survivorship is a neuroscience problem as much as an oncologic one.

    What it took to learn Two things
    • Reading clinical, quality-of-life, and health-economics literature closely enough to synthesize across them rather than summarize each in turn.
    • Naming the limits of an evidence base, from cross-sectional designs and small single-center samples to low response rates and the absence of pre-treatment baselines.

    When Prediction Methods Do Not Transfer: Four Criteria, With Checkpoint-Treated Melanoma Survivorship as a Worked Case

    Bhadra S, Pichappan S, Vatsa A

    In preparation for Health Sciences Review · Elsevier

    Checkpoint blockade has produced a population of melanoma survivors presenting with fatigue, low mood, and cognitive slowing, where six separate mechanisms can each produce all three alone and nothing in the presentation says which is at work. The review asks whether prediction methods built in other populations can move into that setting, testing each against four criteria: mechanism correspondence, reversibility, surveillance context, and feature availability. Six candidate methods fail in ways target data can repair. A seventh clinical requirement, attributing a neuropsychiatric presentation to an underlying endocrine injury, has no candidate method at all, and a simulation of one-directional label noise shows why apparent discrimination can rise while the model itself is unchanged.

    I wrote the manuscript and ran the first-pass screening and data extraction. The second reader handled revisions and shared the blinded dual risk-of-bias appraisal and the criteria reliability assessment where those needed two people.

    What it took to learn Six things
    • Survival analysis from the ground up, from censoring and hazards to the Cox partial likelihood read as a ranking loss over risk sets.
    • PROBAST and CHARMS as instruments with their own rules and signaling questions, not checklists to skim.
    • Positive-unlabeled learning, and why a naive unbiased estimator overfits flexible model families.
    • Monte Carlo design, meaning how many replications buy how much precision, and how to size an effect against its own standard deviation before quoting it.
    • Gwet’s AC1, and why raw percentage agreement is not a reliability statistic.
    • Reading a paper closely enough to notice that its own numbers disagree with each other.
    Method notes Nine
    1. Positive-unlabeled, not noisy labelsAn endocrinopathy that was never detected is not recorded as some other mechanism. It is recorded as no mechanism at all, so the negative class is a mixture of true negatives and undetected positives. That makes this positive-unlabeled learning rather than symmetric label noise, and it changes which estimators can be trusted.
    2. What the simulation actually doesEach replication draws two independent cohorts of 5,000, with eight covariates split into four a survivorship clinic can measure and four it cannot. The outcome comes from a logistic model over a weighted linear combination of those covariates, with weights alternating in sign from 1.4 down to 0.2 and carrying interaction, quadratic, and sinusoidal terms, and the intercept set by quantile matching so marginal prevalence is exact rather than approximate. Six prevalence levels taken from the clinical literature (0.024 to 0.681), two noise rates, two detection regimes, and two model classes give 48 conditions, each run 100 times.
    3. Two learner classes, on purposeLogistic regression and histogram gradient boosting are both run, because a naive unbiased positive-unlabeled estimator is known to overfit flexible model families. Of the 48 conditions, the five that fall outside two Monte Carlo standard deviations are all in the flexible learner, which is the behavior that prediction anticipates.
    4. Detection sets the direction of the biasPresent mechanisms are missed with probability alpha, and absent ones are never recorded as present. Under constant detection, discrimination against the recorded label falls. Under surveillance-dependent detection it rises for the rarer mechanisms, while discrimination against the true mechanism barely moves in either regime. The apparent performance is a property of how patients are watched, not of the model.
    5. Error measured, not assumedThree ways of cutting the screening load were tested and all three were rejected on measured cost. One excluded only 38 records, one lost four relevant papers in a sample of 40 exclusions, and one returned more records than the query it was meant to reduce. A recall check against eight known-eligible studies scored 7 of 8 before a correction and 8 of 8 after. A second independent screen of 250 records puts false exclusion at 3.3% overall and 0.8% on the melanoma arm.
    6. A correction that works for one learner and not the otherDividing the calibration intercept by one minus the noise rate recovers the logistic model almost exactly, moving it from +0.22 to +0.00 at a noise rate of one seventh. The same correction leaves the boosted model at +1.38, having started at +1.58. An analytic fix derived for a well-specified linear model does not carry to a flexible one, which is the sort of thing usually assumed rather than checked.
    7. Effects sized against the noise floorThe Monte Carlo standard deviation across the simulation has a median of 0.007 and a maximum of 0.014. Calibration drift exceeds that by an order of magnitude and is quoted with numbers. The rise in discrimination under surveillance-dependent detection sits within a small factor of it, so it is reported directionally with no magnitude attached, rather than dressed up as a result.
    8. Agreement reported as AC1, and reported lowTwo assessors applied the four criteria across twelve signaling questions and agreed on 102 of 144 answers, giving a Gwet AC1 of 0.58. The weakest criterion was reversibility, which is the one this review contributes, at 0.35 as administered and 0.42 after rewording. Measuring the reliability of a new instrument, and publishing the number when it comes out weak, is not the usual practice.
    9. Published numbers that disagree with themselvesSeveral appraised papers report figures that do not reconcile, including a cohort whose sex counts sum to eight fewer than its stated total, an eight-year AUC given as one value in the text and another in the paper’s own figure, and three mutually inconsistent sets of cross-validated discrimination for the same three timepoints. Each is reported rather than quietly corrected, since the primary data are not available.

  1. 4.1 Computational genomics · self-directed
    1. ProtocolVersion 2.0
    2. RevisedAfter external feedback
    3. DraftedShort manuscript
    4. AnalysisNot started
    Paused

    Paused while the surgical work takes priority. No patient cohort has been analyzed.

    Composition-conditional prognostic signature audit

    Published gene signatures predict survival in gastric cancer, but a tumor sample is a mixture of malignant, fibroblast, immune, and endothelial cells, so a score can rise because the mixture shifted rather than because any cancer cell changed state. The closest prior audit found eleven published EMT and stemness signatures to be dominated by stromal signal, and never examined gastric cancer. Gastric is the right place to extend it, since it is not a proliferation-informative cancer, which leaves composition as the decisive alternative, and its clinically used mesenchymal subtype genuinely carries both more stroma and EMT-shifted cancer cells at once. The estimand is therefore the signature’s hazard coefficient written as a curve over composition rather than as a single number, and that curve is then used to explain why such coefficients fail to transport between cohorts.

    • A cohort-stratified Cox model in which the signature coefficient varies over composition, alongside clinical covariates and a proliferation term, specified for TCGA-STAD and the ACRG cohort.
    • A signature registry fixed in advance and never refit, covering the Kreis eleven-panel set, the ACRG 71-gene set, Hallmark EMT, and lineage-identity sets.
    • An M0 to M4 model ladder, with incremental information over a composition-only model as the primary test and composition dependence of the coefficient as the secondary one.
    • A third cohort sealed for a single-pass confirmation, three-way leave-one-cohort-out validation for transport, and a gene-level deletion audit against size-matched random sets as falsification.
    • R
    • Python
    • Survival analysis
    • Deconvolution
    What it took to learn Five things
    • Compositional data analysis, covering the simplex, log-ratio geometry, and why the constraint breaks ordinary regression.
    • Varying-coefficient models and penalized splines, including how the penalty matrix trades smoothness against fit.
    • Non-collapsibility of the hazard ratio, and standardized absolute risk and restricted mean survival time as the scale that survives it.
    • Deconvolution as constrained least squares, and attenuation bias when a covariate is itself an estimate.
    • Where the field’s ceiling sits, so the claim could be sized against it rather than against a leaderboard.
    Method notes Seven
    1. Compositions are not vectors in the ordinary senseA composition lives on a simplex, four fractions constrained to sum to one, so the coordinates are not free and ordinary regression on them is ill-posed. The model works in isometric log-ratio coordinates, z equal to V transpose times the log of pi, where V is an orthonormal contrast basis for the plane orthogonal to the all-ones direction. Zeros have to be replaced before the logarithm, which is a modeling decision rather than a formatting one.
    2. The varying coefficient compiles into feature crossesLetting the coefficient vary over composition is not a second model bolted onto the first. Writing beta as a spline in the log-ratio coordinates turns the term into products of the signature score with each basis function, so a varying-coefficient Cox fit is an ordinary Cox fit on a wider design matrix. The basis is centered and QR-conditioned, because the raw crosses are badly scaled.
    3. The penalty is a quadratic form, and it has to beThe spline is fitted against a roughness penalty, lambda times theta transpose Omega theta, so the coefficient curve stays smooth instead of chasing a handful of events. Against an events-per-parameter budget of roughly ten to fifteen, that penalty is what keeps the model estimable at all.
    4. Inference on a curve, not on a numberA varying coefficient needs a simultaneous band rather than pointwise error bars, built by the delta method from a transpose Sigma a across the evaluation grid, with curvature summarized as the integral of the squared second derivative. Nesting is tested by likelihood ratio, which is exactly why a ridge-penalized fit is never allowed into the test.
    5. Composition is estimated, not observedThe mixture fractions come from a non-negative least squares fit of bulk expression against a single-cell reference, run through two deconvolution methods plus a methylation-based one so the composition estimate is not a single point of failure. Variance inflation is checked directly, where an exact dependency surfaces as an infinite factor rather than merely a large one.
    6. A ceiling that calibrates the claimLarge re-evaluations of published signatures find concordance plateauing near 0.80 whatever the algorithm, and surrogate gene sets with unrelated or even opposite biology match published signatures on performance. Both facts are stated up front, because together they bound what any integrated model here could honestly promise, and they are why the question is about meaning rather than leaderboard position.
    7. Two traps, written down before the dataMeasurement error in the composition estimate attenuates the coefficient by a signal-to-noise factor, so a flat curve can mean no effect or a noisy sensor, and those two have to be told apart. Separately, the hazard ratio is non-collapsible, so a coefficient can move under adjustment with no confounding present at all, which is why transport is judged on standardized absolute risk and restricted mean survival time rather than on the coefficient itself.
